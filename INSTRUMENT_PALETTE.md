# INSTRUMENT_PALETTE.md: what each ohm can sound like

Owner: Resonance Music Theory. Readers: Game Dev (builds it), Resonance (checks it). Engine: `/workspace/resonance2`.
Format: the `palette` block below follows "Format for Music Theory" in `PLAN_INSTRUMENTS.md` exactly; the game loads only that block.

## 1. What this is

Built from `/workspace/resonance2/SUMMARY.md` (the main reference; it wins over any other source) and
`WILBOW_VERBATIM.md` for detail. Every value here that SUMMARY leaves unanswered is a proposal: Game Dev builds it as a
dev-panel switch with this value as the default, and Wilbow can change it.

This file is the map of the sound space: 73 landmark instruments spread as widely as possible (strings, winds, bars,
bells, drums, voices, synths, the elements, world sounds and sampled sources), each one tuned, ranged and tagged with
the musical traditions that favour it. One recipe holds synth, effects and modulation and nothing is ohm-only, so any
row can be played by an ohm, the planet, the ground or the player.

The rows are **landmarks, not categories**. Genes give an ohm leanings and tone; instruments are learned or picked up,
and the player can assign or sculpt any sound (SUMMARY "All of the sound tools"). Section 6 gives the data for that.

Data notes for the build:
- Each row is instrument data only; it does not change how jams, harmony or the shared music state work.
- Old-game references (`old_game`) are examples to reuse, not requirements.
- Taps sound instantly; no row adds latency before its attack.
- Cost: about 32 live voices; settled songs become loops, and far voices merge into ensemble voices and then one
  background layer (SUMMARY, under discussion). The ensemble group of a row is its `family`. Instrument choice runs at
  hatch, learn, pickup or edit and is cached; no per-frame palette work for off-screen ohms.
- A new sound is a new row; a new tradition is a new key in `trad`.

Words used (explained once):
- **Partial / overtone**: one of the pure frequencies a sound is made of. **Harmonic** partials sit at whole-number
  multiples of the note (1, 2, 3...), which sounds clearly pitched; **inharmonic** partials do not (bells, bars, drums).
- **Exciter**: what starts the sound (pluck, strike, scrape, bow, blow, voice). **Resonator**: what rings (string, tube,
  bar or membrane modes, formant filter). **Engine**: a synth method (VA analog, FM, WT wavetable, ADD additive, PD phase
  distortion, FOLD wavefolding, RING ring modulation, GRAN granular).
- **Attack**: time from silence to full level. **Decay**: how long it rings. **Sustain**: whether it holds while held.
- **Formant**: a fixed resonance peak, like a vowel or a body, that stays put while the note changes.
- **Cents (¢)**: 1/100 of a semitone. **MIDI**: note number; 60 = middle C (261.6 Hz), 69 = A 440. The om defaults to 64 Hz (MIDI ≈ 35.9); one om or each planet's own, by context (SUMMARY 5d).
- **Centroid**: the "centre of mass" of a spectrum; high centroid = bright.
- **Ombak**: the slow beating of two gamelan instruments tuned a few Hz apart, on purpose.

Extra keys in each row (ignored by the loader unless Game Dev wires them): `trad` (tradition weights 0..1), `tuning`
(how the pitch is checked), `pitch_ratio` (which partial sits on the note when it is not partial 1), `src` (where the
sound was asked for: `WV:n` = line n of `/workspace/softsound/WILBOW_VERBATIM.md`), `old_game` (matching code in
`/workspace/softsound/www/js/`, file:line), `use` (world sounds), `source: "sample"` (the row plays a recorded
slice instead of an oscillator; sampling is in SUMMARY as "synthesis plus some sampling (still discussing)").

## 3. Distinctness map

Six axes describe how far apart two instruments sound. Each is computed once per row (cache at load) from the row data:

| axis | from | low | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| A attack | `env.att_s`, exciter | bowed, pads, singing bowl | plucks, mallets, drums |
| S sustain | `env.sus`, `modal.decay_s` | clap, wood_clave, kick | organ, drones, pads |
| C centroid | `filter.cut_hz`, `modal.bright`, `noise.color` | sub_bass, earth_rumble, guqin | shaker_hat, glockenspiel, fm_glass |
| H harmonicity | partials vs whole numbers, engine | gong, ring_bell, fire_crackle | sine_tone, organ, bowed strings |
| R register | centre of `range_midi` | kick, kargyraa, sub_bass | glockenspiel, shaker_hat |
| V motion | `vib`, `wt_scan`, `tech.repeat` | marimba, organ (no vibrato) | moving_pad, erhu, santur rolls |

Distance = weighted sum of axis gaps (A 1, S 1, C 1.2, H 1, R .8, V .6), each 0..1.
- **When a jam forms**, compute the pairwise distance of the members' blended voices once and cache it. Recompute only when
  a member joins, leaves, learns a sound or is edited.
- Use it as a **weight, never a ban**: when an ohm picks which of its voices or variants to use in that jam, multiply each
  option by `0.6 + 0.4 * min(1, d / 1.5)` where d is its distance to the nearest other member. Pairs closer than 0.5 get a
  small register or brightness nudge (one octave or ±20% cutoff) instead of being refused.
- `distinct_from` in each row lists the two rows it is most easily confused with; the checklist tests those pairs.
- At spawn, the old game's spread check (`surface.js:180-196`, `inst.js:136-139` identity/family) is the model: same idea,
  same cheap once-only cost.

## 4. Tuning

### 4.1 Tolerance per family (measured on the rendered sound, after 50 ms)

| family | what is measured | tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| sustained harmonic (bowed, blown, voice, organ, pads, bass) | fundamental | ±5 ¢ (pads, choir, accordion: centre of the detuned group ±10-15 ¢) |
| plucked and struck strings | fundamental after 50 ms (a pluck starts slightly sharp) | ±8 ¢ |
| tuned bars, tines, pans, FM bells | the perceived partial (`pitch_ratio`, default partial 1) | ±10 ¢ |
| bells, gongs, singing bowl | perceived partial; slow beats allowed | ±10-20 ¢ |

| unpitched (clap, shaker_hat, fire_crackle, motion_rattle) | none | - |
| elements with a pitch (wind whistle, water drop, earth hum) | landing pitch | lands on a scale degree ±40 ¢ |

Each row's `tune_tol_cents` is the number the test uses.

### 4.2 Jam-tuned strings

Strings tied by joining ohms follow V08_SPEC §D (`/workspace/softsound/V08_SPEC.md:117`): each string snaps to a note of
the jam's current scale, each strictly above the previous degree, and retunes with a 120 ms glide when the chord or key
changes. Plucked rows (`part: string`) are the default voice for those strings; their `range_midi` caps the top string.

Data rules:
- Every row carries one pitch per note; the tradition's cents table decides which pitch. Rows marked "maqam cents exact"
  or "set" must receive the exact cents from `music.js` TUNINGS, never a 12-TET rounding.
- Planets and regions are blends with no borders (SUMMARY v0.0.12-13), so a place holds a mix of traditions, not one.
  Instrument weight: `w = 1 + 2.5 * Σ_t trad[t] * share(t)`, where `share(t)` is that tradition's share in the
  blend at the ohm's spot (shares sum to 1). Rows with no entry keep `w = 1`, so they still appear; rows with an empty
  `trad` (elements, world, sample rows) are tradition-neutral. How maqam, raga and pentatonic flavours enter regions
  (Q320) and how strongly place pulls harmony (Q322) are open: 2.5 is the proposal default.
- Pure (just-intonation) tuning is a v0.0.2 switch under judgment by ear; rows tagged `track` follow whichever tuning
  is active.
- Gamelan detune: ombak beats at a constant 5-7 Hz across the register (detune in Hz, not cents; see §8 gap 6).

### 4.4 Overtones at small scales

Zooming into tiny worlds reveals overtones (SUMMARY 5c, answered A). Each row's `overtones` text and `modal.partials`
(or harmonic series for string, tube and osc rows) are the data for that: at small scales, play a row's partials as

## 6. Genes, learning and sculpting: where an ohm's sound comes from

SUMMARY: genes give leanings and tone; instruments are learned or picked up; an ohm's role grows from what it plays; the
player can assign or sculpt its sound. Whether "an ohm keeps its own instrument" or learns instruments is still open;
the proposal is its own tone from its genes, sitting under what it learns. The data below serves that proposal and
keeps every option switchable.

1. **Point in sound space.** Build a vector from the ohm's synth genes and body (size, float, round, spiky, wide,
   lobes, modal fam weights string/membrane/bar, `bright`, `decay`, `inharm`). Each row's own vector comes from its `body`
   plus its exc/res/engine values. Cache all row vectors at load.
2. **Gene tone.** For each row: `w = exp(-d² / 0.18) × tradition weight (§4.3) × distinctness weight (§3)`. Blend the two
   highest, add the seeded per-ohm spread (±10% on continuous fields, ±1 step on discrete ones). This is the ohm's tone:
   it colours whatever it plays (filter, brightness, decay, vibrato, body thump) and is what you recognise it by.
3. **Uniform genes must not average out.** Draw the starting point from a mixture: 70% near a single row, 30% between
   two rows, across the full gene range, so a planet does not sound like one similar mallet.
4. **Learned and picked-up instruments.** Each is a row reference with a strength 0..1 that grows with experience
   (jams, songs, playing with the environment, teaching, offspring). It plays through the gene tone. A drum ohm growing
   into a full kit is gene tone + learned drum rows (drum kits themselves are on hold, B36).
5. **Role from what it plays.** The rows an ohm plays most become its most likely voices; no row is fixed to a role.
6. **Player assign and sculpt.** Assigning a row or sculpting by touch (clay stage, mouth control, physical controls)
   writes directly to the ohm's sound and becomes its new starting point.
7. **Recipes are seeds that can be bred.** A child recipe = per-field blend of two parents' recipes plus seeded spread;
   offspring also inherit learned rows at reduced strength.
8. **Slow change (proposal switch).** The gene tone may shift toward voices the ohm plays with often: up to 2% of the
   distance per settled song, capped at 25%, so it stays recognisable. A rare, very experienced ohm "can change
   anything", so the cap lifts for it.
9. **Fully random voices.** Untouched clay and default spawns pick a random point anywhere in the space across all
   engines (V08_SPEC §P, `V08_SPEC.md:1188`); drawn instruments do the same when they return (on hold, F5).
10. All of this runs at hatch, learn, pickup, song end or edit. Never per frame.

## 8. Gaps in `www/js/dsp/inst.js` (resonance2) this palette needs closed

1. **Waveguide tuning drifts flat.** Measured with the exact-sine floor turned off (P.osc = 0) at 110 / 440 / 1320 Hz:
   pluck string at low cutoff −6 / −24 / +10 ¢; mid cutoff −2 / −10 / −31 ¢; blown tube at low cutoff −8 / −34 / −100 ¢.
   Cause: delay `L = SR/f` (inst.js:27) ignores the loop filter's phase delay (inst.js:56), the interpolation and tanh.
   Fix: subtract the loop filter's phase delay at f0 from L (or allpass fractional delay). The `P.osc` floor of .15
   (synth.js:15) hides the error but puts the same sine under every voice and makes them sound alike.
2. Inst voice modal bank has 4 modes (inst.js:25); bells and gongs need 6-8 (`modal.partials` up to 8).
3. No tuned-bar family (1:4:10) in recipe FAM; synth.js MARIMBA `[1, 3.99, 10.65, 4.0]` repeats a partial; use the
   `marimba` row.
4. No string stiffness (stretched partials) for santur and harp; no jawari bridge buzz for sitar; no gourd buzz for
   balafon or tine sizzle for mbira (a short noise band triggered by amplitude covers all three).
5. Tube is closed at both ends with inverted feedback (odd harmonics only): right for clarinet and panpipe, wrong for
   flutes. Flutes use `part: blow` with a string-type (open) loop, as set in the rows.
6. Detune is in cents; gamelan ombak needs a constant beat in Hz.
7. Vibrato starts at once; voices need a delay (about 300 ms fade-in).
8. Formants are fixed per note; overtone singing needs a narrow formant that tracks harmonic 6-13 of the drone.
9. tract.js (vocal tract) not ported; the five voice rows and spoken names need it.
10. The modal 'note' path has no pitch envelope (kick drop, talking drum slide, water drop rise).
11. drums.js HITS (kick to bell, 11 hits with strokes) not ported; the drum rows here replace them as voices.
12. Samples: slice playback exists (sample bank, `inst.js:17-20`, `inst.js:64`), but granular reads only the world's own
    recent sound (`inst.js:76`); `sample_grain` needs grains from a bank slice. Chop, reverse and stutter of slices by ohms
    are not there yet.

## 9. Review checklist for Game Dev builds

Automatable (render offline with the node harness, one note at a time, no effects):
1. JSON: the `palette` block parses; every `id` unique; every `distinct_from` id exists; every value inside the ranges in
   PLAN_INSTRUMENTS.md.
2. Pitch: for every pitched row, render MIDI lo+5, middle, hi−5; measure the fundamental (or `pitch_ratio` partial) by
   autocorrelation after 50 ms; error ≤ `tune_tol_cents`.
3. Same test with `P.osc = 0` for all string, tube and blow rows (catches gap 1).
4. Distinctness: for each `distinct_from` pair at the same note, spectral centroid differs by ≥ 15% or attack time by
   ≥ 2× or decay by ≥ 2×.
5. Spread: hatch 200 random ohms; no single row is nearest for more than 12%; every family appears; mean pairwise
   distance (§3) ≥ 0.6.
6. Traditions: at a spot where tradition t has share 1, the top-5 rows for t appear at least 2× as often as on a neutral planet, and
   at least 25% of ohms still come from rows without a `t` entry.
7. Scales: each tradition's cents table plays back within ±2 ¢ of the numbers in §4.3 on a sustained row.
8. Gamelan: two detuned saron voices beat at 5-7 Hz at both ends of the range.
